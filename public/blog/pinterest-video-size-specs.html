Pinterest Video Size and Specs: What Actually Matters

For creators Published: November 2026 6 min read By Manikant Yadav

Pinterest will accept almost any video you throw at it. That's the problem. A clip that looks great on your phone can show up with its headline cut off, or come back blurry because it was exported at the wrong size. Below are the specs worth caring about, the ones you can ignore, and where to put text so it survives.

The "accepts" column comes from Pinterest's product specs page. The right-hand column is our recommendation, based on what Pinterest actually serves back when KlickPint fetches a video pin (the largest version is 1080p). Pinterest changes its specs now and then, so check their page before a big campaign.

Aspect ratio: pick a tall one

Pinterest is a vertical feed. A 16:9 landscape video technically uploads fine, but in a two-column grid on a phone it shrinks to a thin strip that nobody stops for. Tall videos take up more of the screen, and on Pinterest screen space is most of the battle.

Which one to choose depends on where else the video is going:

  • 2:3 (1000 × 1500) fits a standard pin exactly. Nothing gets cropped anywhere on Pinterest. If the video is made only for Pinterest, use this.
  • 9:16 (1080 × 1920) is the same file you'd post to Reels, TikTok or Shorts. It works on Pinterest, with one catch covered in the next section.
  • 4:5 and 1:1 are fine if you already have them from Instagram. They just use less of the screen than 2:3.

Where to put text in a 9:16 video

A full 9:16 video is taller than a 2:3 pin. Depending on where someone sees it, Pinterest may show the whole thing or fit it into a 2:3 shape, which trims a strip from the top and bottom. On top of that, the pin title and buttons sit over the lower part of the frame in some views.

The fix is easy once you know about it. Keep your headline, captions and anyone's face in the middle of the frame. Leave the top and bottom of a 9:16 video for background. If you edit in CapCut or Canva, drop a 2:3 rectangle on a separate layer as a guide and delete it before export.

Resolution: 1080 wide, no more

Export at 1080 pixels wide. Pinterest doesn't publish a required minimum, but when we fetch a pin it usually offers versions up to 1080p, and uploading 4K doesn't add a bigger version. It just makes your upload take longer.

Going lower does hurt. Pinterest only creates versions up to the size you uploaded. Upload a 720p file and there will never be a 1080p version of that pin, for viewers or for anyone saving it later. Our quality guide explains why the list of available qualities changes from pin to pin.

Length: shorter than you think

The 15-minute limit is generous, but people scroll Pinterest looking for ideas, not to sit through a long video. Recipe clips, outfit try-ons and before-and-after edits usually say everything in 6 to 30 seconds. If your video is a full tutorial, show the finished result in the first two seconds and save the steps for the rest. That first frame decides whether someone keeps watching.

Sound is optional, captions aren't

Videos autoplay in the feed with the sound off. If the message depends on someone talking, most viewers will miss it. Put the key line on screen as text: "3-ingredient pasta", "Before / after", "Step 2: sand the edges". Burn captions into the video if the voice-over matters.

Frequently asked questions

Can I upload a landscape (16:9) video to Pinterest?

Yes, it will upload and play. It shows up small in the feed, though. If the video only exists in landscape, consider cropping a vertical version for Pinterest, even if it means losing the edges.

Why does my uploaded video look worse than the original?

Pinterest re-compresses every upload. Starting from a clean 1080p H.264 file gives it the most to work with. Exporting at a low bitrate first, then letting Pinterest compress again, is what produces the blocky, smeared look.

How do I get my own video back from Pinterest?

Pinterest has no export button for your videos. If you lost the original file, paste your pin's link into KlickPint and download the highest quality listed. That is Pinterest's best copy of your upload, so it's worth keeping the original file safe next time.

Tip: Make the cover image a frame from the finished result. Someone scrolling past can tell what they'll get from a good "after" shot. A title card tells them nothing.

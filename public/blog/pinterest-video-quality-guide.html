Pinterest Video Quality Explained: 1080p vs 720p vs 480p vs 360p

Guide Updated: November 2026 4 min read

KlickPint shows you every quality Pinterest stored for a given pin, and the list isn't always the same: sometimes it's four options, sometimes it's one. This guide covers what those numbers mean, why the list varies, and how to pick the right one instead of grabbing the biggest file by habit.

What the Resolution Number Measures

1080p, 720p, 480p, and 360p all refer to the video's vertical pixel count: 1080 pixels tall for Full HD, down to 360 for the lowest tier. More pixels means more visual detail, especially noticeable on a larger screen or when you pause on a frame. On a phone screen at arm's length, the practical difference between 1080p and 720p is much smaller than the file size difference suggests.

Rough File Size by Quality

Exact size depends on the clip's length and how much motion is in it, but for a typical 20–30 second Pinterest video, here's what to expect:

QualityTypical Size (30s clip)Good For
1080p8–15 MBRe-posting, editing, big screens
720p4–8 MBEveryday viewing and sharing
480p2–4 MBSaving storage space
360p1–2 MBSlow or metered mobile data

Why Some Pins Only Offer 480p or Lower

KlickPint can only offer what Pinterest stored, and Pinterest can only store what the original uploader provided. If a creator uploaded their video at 720p, there is no real 1080p version anywhere. Pinterest doesn't invent extra detail that was never there. So if you're only seeing lower-quality options for a specific pin, that's a ceiling set by the original upload, not a limitation of the downloader.

Which Quality Should You Pick?

1

Re-posting to Instagram, TikTok, or YouTube Shorts

Go with 1080p if it's available. Those platforms re-compress everything you upload, and starting from a higher-quality source gives their compression more detail to preserve. After their compression, a 1080p source usually looks sharper than a 720p one, even if the two looked the same on your phone.

2

Just watching it later, offline

720p is the sweet spot. It looks almost identical to 1080p on a phone or tablet screen and takes up roughly half the storage.

3

Saving a lot of videos, or low on storage

480p holds up fine for casual viewing and keeps your device's storage in check if you're archiving dozens of clips.

4

On a slow connection or limited mobile data

360p downloads fastest and costs the least data, which is reasonable if the content itself (a recipe, a tutorial with text overlays) matters more than visual polish.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why isn't 1080p available for some pins?

Pinterest only stores qualities up to whatever the original uploader provided. If someone uploaded a video at 720p, Pinterest never generates a fake 1080p version, so there is nothing to download.

Does higher quality always mean a bigger file?

Generally yes, but bitrate and length matter more than resolution alone. A short, simple 1080p clip can be smaller than a long, busy 480p one, since motion and detail in the footage affect file size as much as resolution does.

What resolution do Instagram and TikTok need?

Both compress video on upload regardless of source quality, but starting from 1080p gives their compression algorithm more detail to work with, so the final result looks noticeably sharper than starting from 480p and having it compressed twice.

Will KlickPint upscale a low-quality video to look like 1080p?

No, and be skeptical of any tool that claims to. Upscaling adds pixels through interpolation, not real detail; it can look smoother but never restores information that wasn't in the original file. KlickPint only offers the genuine qualities Pinterest stored.

Tip: If you're not sure which to pick, download 720p by default. It's the best balance of quality and file size for almost every use case, and it's very rarely the wrong call.

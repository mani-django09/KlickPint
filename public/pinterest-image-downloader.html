Why your saved Pinterest images look blurry

Pinterest keeps several copies of every image and shows you the smallest one that fits your screen. The grid uses 236 or 474 pixel copies, and an opened pin usually shows 736 pixels. Right-click "Save image" or a screenshot gives you one of those. The file the creator actually uploaded sits in a folder called /originals/, which the Pinterest app never links to.

KlickPint reads the pin's data and goes straight to the original. If the button says the original is only 600 pixels wide, that is what the creator uploaded, and there is nothing larger anywhere. Our full guide to Pinterest image sizes also shows how to find the original by hand on a computer.

FAQ

Questions about downloading Pinterest images

How do I download a Pinterest image in full size?
Copy the pin's link, paste it above and choose Original. That is the uploaded file at its full width and height. The button shows the exact dimensions before you download.
Pinterest has its own "Download image" option. Why use this?
Use Pinterest's option if it's there and you're happy with the result. It isn't on every pin, and it doesn't tell you the file's size. KlickPint shows the dimensions first and also works for GIFs and videos.
Can I download every image in a carousel pin?
Not yet. For a carousel (multi-image) pin, KlickPint saves the cover image. Open the pin on Pinterest to save the other slides.
Can I download a whole board?
No, one pin at a time on purpose. Bulk-copying someone's board is a different thing from saving an image you like, and Pinterest blocks that kind of traffic quickly.
What if the pin is a GIF or a video?
Paste it anyway. GIF pins give you a GIF button, and video pins give you MP4 qualities up to 1080p. There's also a dedicated Pinterest GIF downloader page with tips for animated pins.
Does it work on iPhone?
Yes. In Safari, the image saves to the Files app (Downloads). Open it there, tap Share and choose Save Image to put it in Photos.
Can I use images I download from Pinterest?
For personal use, such as wallpapers, mood boards and reference, generally yes. Posting them as your own, selling prints or using them in marketing needs the owner's permission. See our plain-English copyright guide.
